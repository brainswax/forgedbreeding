# DAIRY_CONCEPTS.md
**ADGA Linear Appraisal concepts, trait scales, and terminology**  
**Source:** [ADGA Linear Appraisal System booklet (2014/2019)](https://adga.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/LABOOKLETALL_19.pdf)  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-27  

Authoritative reference for how Linear Appraisal (LA) traits are defined and scored.  
**Use this file whenever writing or interpreting LA reports** so trait polarity, ideal direction, and terminology stay consistent with ADGA — do not invent scales or reverse biological extremes.

---

## How to use this document

1. Before describing a trait as “good,” “weak,” “flat,” “steep,” “high,” or “low,” check the scale and biological meaning below.
2. Prefer **full ADGA trait names** in prose (e.g. Medial Suspensory Ligament, Rear Legs Side View). Abbreviations are fine in tables and Final Score shorthand (`VEVV 88`).
3. Linear scores are on a **1–50** observational scale (biological extremes), not a “higher is always better” scale for every trait.
4. If this file and a prior report conflict on trait polarity, **this file wins**; correct the report.
5. Follow the **language rules** below (especially breeding terminology).

---

## Language rules

### Breeding — not mating

In all reports, prompts, and docs:

- Use **breeding** / **breedings** — never **mating** / **matings**.
- Use the verb **breed** — never **mate**.
- Prefer phrases like: breeding, proposed breeding, recommended breeding, breeding pair, breeding choice, breed to, breed with.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| mating | breeding |
| matings | breedings |
| mate (verb) | breed |
| mate her to X | breed her to X |
| recommended mating | recommended breeding |
| prospective mating | prospective breeding |
| test mating | test breeding |

These words are house style even if other sources say “mating.”

## System overview

ADGA linear appraisal:

- Evaluates each trait **individually**, from one biological extreme to the other.
- Uses traits with economic importance that are at least moderately heritable.
- Assigns linear scores on a **50-point range** for consistent discrimination.
- Includes **13 primary linear traits** + **1 secondary linear trait**.
- Adds **eight structural/functional areas** scored with letter ratings.
- Allows up to **three remarks and/or defects**.
- Ends with **four major categories** (three for bucks) and a **Final Score**.

**Database / sire summaries:** Only linear trait scores and Final Score enter the computerized LA database used for sire summaries. Structural letters, remarks, and defects are for the herd owner.

Appraisers score linear traits **without regard to age, management, environment, or stage of lactation**. Structural areas may consider management/environment. Major categories and Final Score may consider age, pregnancy/lactation stage, and production when effects are obvious.

## Score types at a glance

| Kind | What it is | Scale |
|------|------------|--------|
| Primary / secondary **linear traits** | Observed biological position | Typically **1–50** (see trait sections) |
| **Structural / functional areas** | Head, shoulders, legs, feet, back, rump, udder texture | Letter: E / V / + / A / F / P |
| **Major categories** | General Appearance, Dairy Strength, Body Capacity, Mammary | Same letters (Mammary = mature does only) |
| **Final Score** | Overall closeness to ideal | Numeric for mature animals (except Poor = `P`); young stock use letters only |

### Letter ratings (structural areas & major categories)

| Letter | Name | Meaning |
|--------|------|---------|
| **E** | Excellent | ≥ 90% of ideal (mature program) |
| **EC** | Extremely Correct | ≥ 90% of ideal (young stock program) |
| **V** | Very Good | 85–89% of ideal |
| **+** | Good Plus | 80–84% of ideal |
| **A** | Acceptable | 70–79% of ideal |
| **F** | Fair | 60–69% of ideal |
| **P** | Poor | < 60% of ideal |

**Note:** ADGA herd score exports sometimes print Good Plus as **`G`** instead of **`+`**. Treat `G` and `+` as the same Good Plus band when composing Final Score shorthand (e.g. `VEEG 85` = V / E / E / G).

### Final Score contribution (judge’s scorecard weights)

**Mature does** (any doe that has ever freshened):

| Category | Weight |
|----------|--------|
| General Appearance | 35% |
| Dairy Strength | 20% |
| Body Capacity | 10% |
| Mammary | 35% |

**Bucks and all young stock:**

| Category | Weight |
|----------|--------|
| General Appearance | 55% |
| Dairy Strength | 30% |
| Body Capacity | 15% |
| Mammary | *(not used)* |

**Permanent scores:** If reappraised in/after the month of the fifth birthday, scores can be raised or stay the same but generally cannot be lowered (exception: dry appraisal on/after fifth birthday later replaced by a milking appraisal).

## Primary linear traits

### Form — Stature

**Meaning:** Distance from level ground to the top of the withers.

**Standard scale (points):**
| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Extremely short (≤ ~26″) |
| 15 | Short |
| 25 | Intermediate (~30″) |
| 35 | Tall |
| ≥45 | Extremely tall (≥ ~34″) |

About **5 points per 1″** height change on the standard scale.

**Miniature breeds:** Stature is recorded in **inches** (nearest ¼″) while biological range data are gathered — not always as a 1–50 point assignment. Reports may still show a stature linear from the herd report; when owner height is known, also use the miniature height→score table in `LA_REPORT_GUIDELINES.md`.

---

### Form — Strength

**Meaning:** Width and depth of chest, width of muzzle, and substance of bone in the front end. Related to sustaining high production and general health.

**Polarity:** Low = narrow/frail; high = wide/strong.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| 5 | Extremely narrow and frail |
| 15 | Narrow and frail |
| 25 | Intermediate width and strength |
| 35 | Very wide and strong |
| 45 | Extremely wide and strong |

Weakness typically **&lt;20**; strength typically **&gt;30**; intermediate **20–30**.

---

### Form — Dairyness

**Meaning:** Length, cleanness and flatness of bone; length and leanness of neck; definition/sharpness of withers; degree of fleshing; femininity/refinement; fineness/texture of skin.

**Polarity:** Low = thick/coarse; high = sharp/angular.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤10 / 5 | Extremely thick and coarse; extremely round bone |
| 15 | Thick and coarse; round bone |
| 25 | Intermediate angularity and flatness of bone |
| 35 | Sharp and angular; clean, flat bone |
| ≥40 / 45 | Extremely sharp and angular; extremely clean, flat bone |

---

### Structure — Rump Angle

**Meaning:** Angle of the rump/pelvis **from hips to pins**, viewed from the side (goat on the move). Affects kidding ease and reproductive-tract drainage; also related to udder length fore-to-rear, fore udder attachment strength, and udder depth.

**Polarity (critical):**
- **Low scores = steep** (pins drop more relative to hips)
- **High scores = level / flat** (toward pins level with hips; extreme levelness can approach inverted)

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 / 5 | Extremely steep from hips to pins |
| 15 | Considerable slope from hips to pins |
| 25 | Moderate slope from hips to pins |
| 35 | Nearly level from hips to pins |
| ≥45 / 45 | Extremely level from hips to pins |

Booklet guidance: steepness often **≤20**; levelness often **≥30**; intermediate slopes roughly **20–30**. About **5 points per 5°** change. A physical rump angle of **50° or more** is assigned **1 point** (extreme steep).

**Report language:**
- Say **steep** for low RA scores; say **level / flat** for high RA scores.
- “Further **flattening**” means moving the score **up** toward 50.

---

### Structure — Rump Width

**Meaning:** Width between the **thurls** (pelvic girdle). Related to kidding ease, general body width, and potential udder width.

**Polarity:** Low = narrow; high = wide.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Extremely narrow through pelvic area (~≤5″ standard) |
| 15 | Narrow |
| 25 | Intermediate (~7″ standard) |
| 35 | Wide |
| ≥45 | Extremely wide (~≥9″ standard) |

About **10 points per 1″** on the standard scale. **Miniature:** proportionately scored / may be recorded in inches while ranges are established.

---

### Structure — Rear Legs, Side View

**Meaning:** Degree of angulation at the hock (side view). Relates to durability of legs and feet.

**Polarity:**
- **Low = posty / straight** in the hock
- **High = sickled / more angled**

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| 5 | Straight-legged (posty) in hock |
| 15 | Nearly straight (posty) |
| 25 | Intermediate angle |
| 35 | Considerably angled (sickled) |
| 45 | Extremely angled (sickled) |

Posty tendency often **≤20**; greater angulation often **≥30**. Intermediate is not “both extremes are equally ideal” — track herd goals; extremes either way can be functional risks.

---

### Mammary — Fore Udder Attachment

**Meaning:** Strength of lateral ligaments extending forward/laterally to the body wall.

**Polarity:** Low = loose; high = snug/strong.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Extremely loose attachment |
| 15 | Loose attachment |
| 25 | Intermediate strength |
| 35 | Strong attachment |
| 45 | Extremely snug and strong attachment |

---

### Mammary — Rear Udder Height

**Meaning:** Height of rear udder attachment (capacity and ability to hold shape over lactations). Scored in proportion to the goat. Not recorded on bucks.

**Polarity:** Low = low attachment; high = high attachment.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Extremely low |
| 15 | Low |
| 25 | Intermediate height |
| 35 | High |
| ≥45 | Extremely high |

Hair/skin texture differences between rear udder and escutcheon help locate the attachment. Young stock program may call this **Escutcheon Height** (inches).

---

### Mammary — Rear Udder Arch

**Meaning:** Width and shape of the rear udder attachment (capacity and ability to hold shape). Evaluated at the same location as rear udder height.

**Polarity:** Low = narrow/pointed; high = wide/curving.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Extremely narrow and pointed arch |
| 15 | Narrow and pointed arch |
| 25 | Intermediate width and curve |
| 35 | Wide and curving arch |
| ≥45 | Extremely wide and curving arch |

Young stock: **Escutcheon Arch**.

---

### Mammary — Medial Suspensory Ligament (MSL)

**Meaning:** Primary support of the udder. Strong MSL keeps teats placed and udder elevated, reducing injury risk. Scored from cleft in the floor of the udder attributable to the MSL.

**Polarity:** Low = weak support / bulging or negative cleft; high = deep cleft / strong support.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Bulging udder floor, negative cleft |
| *(low teens)* | Flat floor / little clear halving |
| ~15–25 | Clearly defined halving and support (~1″ cleft ≈ 25 in booklet prose) |
| 35 | Deep cleft |
| ≥45 | Extreme cleft (~3″+ ≈ 45+) |

About **10 points per 1″** of cleft. **Higher MSL is generally desirable** for support and longevity (within functional extremes).

---

### Mammary — Udder Depth

**Meaning:** Vertical distance between udder floor and point of hock (rear leg set normally). Some depth is needed for capacity; extreme depth raises injury/mastitis risk.

**Polarity (important):**  
- **Low scores = deep / pendulous** (floor toward or below hocks)  
- **High scores = shallow / high** (floor well above hocks)

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Floor well below hocks; pendulous (~≥2″ below; ≥3″ below → 1 point) |
| 15 | Floor below point of hock |
| 20 | Floor at point of hock |
| 25 | Floor above hock (~2″ above = intermediate in prose) |
| 35 | Floor well above hock |
| ≥45 | Floor extremely high; shallow udder (~≥6″ above) |

**Miniature:** proportionately scored. Do not call a high Udder Depth score “deep.”

---

### Mammary — Teat Placement, Rear View

**Meaning:** Teat location on the udder half from the rear (ease of milking; injury risk). Position = center of teat at attachment.

**Polarity:** Low = too far to the outside; high = closer toward midline (more ideal placement on the half).

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| 5 | Extremely wide — outside of udder half |
| 15 | On outside of udder half |
| 25 | About ⅔ of the way out on the udder half |
| 35 | Midway between ⅓ and ⅔ out |
| 45 | About ⅓ of the way out on the udder half |

Outside-third placements are typically **&lt;25** (**wide** placement); more medial useful placements **&gt;25** (**narrower / close** to midline on the half).

**Report language:** Use **wide** and **narrow** (or **close**) for Teat Placement.

---

### Mammary — Teat Diameter

**Meaning:** Diameter at the base where teat meets udder (rear view). Reflects delineation and ease of milking.

**Polarity:** Low = narrow; high = wide. Extremes either way can be undesirable for function.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Extremely narrow (~≤½″) |
| 15 | Narrow |
| 25 | Intermediate (~1½″) |
| 35 | Wide |
| ≥45 | Extremely wide (~≥2½″) |

About **10 points per ½″**.

---

## Secondary linear trait

### Mammary — Rear Udder, Side View (RUSV)

**Meaning:** Shape of the rear udder extending behind the rear leg when legs are square beneath the doe. Indicates rear-udder milk capacity.

**Polarity:** Low = flat / little capacity; high = rounded / bulging / protruding.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Extremely flat; lacks capacity |
| 15 | Flat |
| 25 | Intermediate fullness |
| 35 | Rounded |
| ≥45 | Extremely rounded, bulging, or protruding |

**Note:** Some ADGA web exports show unusual 1–4 values in this column; treat extreme outliers as possible data artifacts and verify before heavy interpretation.

## Major categories (what appraisers consider)

### General Appearance (GA)
Total structure: head, shoulder blades, back, loin, rump, legs, feet. Attractive blending, vigor, style, carriage, walk. Strength, upstandingness, stretch, sound feet/legs, level topline, wide/level rump, smooth blending support E/V.

### Dairy Strength (DS)
Bone structure (neck, withers, ribs, thighs), flanks, angularity, openness, fleshing, animation, skin/hair. Sharp, angular, refined bone, lean blending neck, open ribbing, incurving thighs → E/V. Coarse, round, heavy → F/P.

### Body Capacity
Length, width, depth with correct shape (feed capacity). Deep supported barrel, sprung ribs widening rearward, large heart girth, wide chest floor → E/V. Narrow/pinched girth, frailness, shallow or cramped ribbing → F/P.

### Mammary System (mature does)
Capacity and shape; rear and fore attachments; texture; teats. Capacious, strongly attached, well-carried, quality udder for production and longevity. Soft, pliable texture; uniform, well-placed teats. Short/bulgy/loose fore, low/narrow/loose/pinched rear, tilted/pendulous → F/P.

## Functional risks & mitigations

Use this section when a breeding or selection package includes an undesirable extreme or a known functional concern. **Herd target bands and owner preferences belong in that herd’s `GOALS.md`** — this section only states biological/functional risks and usual mitigations.

### Rump Angle

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Very steep** (booklet: steepness often ≤20) | Can affect kidding ease, reproductive-tract drainage, and related udder topography | Select toward a more moderate/level angle over generations; pair with adequate **Rump Width** for pelvic capacity |
| **Very level / flat** (booklet: levelness often ≥30; extreme toward inverted) | Relatively level pelves can increase kidding difficulty, especially if the pelvis is also narrow | Prefer adequate-to-wide **Rump Width** (thurls) when angle is in the level range; avoid stacking extreme flatness with narrow rumps |
| **Moving steep → more level** | Often desirable structurally when leaving an extreme steep | Treat as improvement toward moderation unless the herd’s goals say otherwise; do **not** invent a risk solely because the score rose from steep toward moderate/level |
| **Further flattening** when already very level | Harder to reverse; can compound kidding risk | Avoid partners that push angle still higher; maintain or improve width |

**Rump Width** is always useful for pelvic capacity. It is also the usual mitigation for kidding risk when Rump Angle is relatively level (~≥30) or flatter.

### Rump Width

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Narrow** | Limits pelvic capacity; weaker kidding buffer; may limit udder width potential | Prefer wider partners; avoid stacking two narrow rumps |

### Medial Suspensory Ligament

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Weak / low cleft** | Udder support fails earlier; teats displace; longevity and Final Score suffer | Prefer partners with stronger MSL (or proven mammary transmitting); avoid stacking two weak-MSL animals |

### Teat Placement (rear) & Teat Diameter

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Wide placement** (low score — too far outside) | Harder milking; higher injury risk | Prefer partners with closer/more medial placement; avoid stacking wide×wide |
| **Extremely narrow or extremely wide diameter** | Milking difficulty; injury or machine fit issues | Prefer intermediate diameters; don’t stack extremes |

### Udder Depth

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Very deep / pendulous** (low score) | Injury, mastitis, and longevity risk | Prefer partners that carry udders higher; strong MSL and attachments help hold shape |

### Rear Legs — Side View

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Posty or sickled extremes** | Foot/leg durability over time (low heritability — slow change) | Prefer intermediate set; track multi-generationally rather than expecting one breeding to “fix” it |

### Strength / Dairyness extremes

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Very frail (low Strength)** | Harder to sustain production and general thrift | Prefer stronger partners; don’t stack frail×frail |
| **Very coarse (low Dairyness)** | Score and dairy character suffer | Prefer sharper, cleaner-boned partners |

### How reports should use this

1. Name the trait risk in plain language (using correct polarity from this file).  
2. Cite the mitigation from this table when one exists.  
3. Apply **herd preferences** (target bands, animals to protect) only from that herd’s `GOALS.md` / roster notes — do not invent them here.

## Quick polarity cheat sheet

| Trait | Low score means | High score means | “Better” direction (typical) |
|-------|-----------------|------------------|------------------------------|
| Stature | Short | Tall | Breed/goals dependent (miniatures!) |
| Strength | Frail/narrow | Wide/strong | Higher usually preferred |
| Dairyness | Coarse/thick | Sharp/angular | Higher usually preferred |
| **Rump Angle** | **Steep** | **Level / flat** | Avoid extremes; flattening = **up** |
| Rump Width | Narrow | Wide | Higher usually preferred |
| Rear Legs Side View | Posty | Sickled | Intermediate often preferred |
| Fore Udder Attachment | Loose | Snug/strong | Higher usually preferred |
| Rear Udder Height | Low | High | Higher usually preferred |
| Rear Udder Arch | Narrow/pointed | Wide/curved | Higher usually preferred |
| Medial Suspensory Ligament | Weak / negative cleft | Deep cleft / support | Higher usually preferred |
| **Udder Depth** | **Deep / pendulous** | **Shallow / high** | Avoid extreme depth (low) |
| Teat Placement Rear | Too far out | More medial on half | Higher usually preferred |
| Teat Diameter | Narrow | Wide | Intermediate often preferred |
| Rear Udder Side View | Flat / low capacity | Bulging / protruding | Intermediate–full; avoid extremes |

## Common hallucination pitfalls (do not do these)

1. **Reversing Rump Angle** — low ≠ flat; high ≠ steep.
2. **Reversing Udder Depth** — high score ≠ deep udder.
3. **Treating every 50-point trait as “higher is always better.”**
4. **Confusing structural Rump letter** with linear Rump Angle / Rump Width.
5. **Assuming bucks have mammary linear scores** — many mammary traits are not recorded on bucks.
6. **Inventing heritabilities** — booklet notes dairy-goat structural h² were historically inferred from cattle; use the herd’s `GOALS.md` priorities rather than fabricating numbers.
7. **Ignoring miniature proportion** — stature/rump width/udder depth may be inches or proportionately scored.
8. **Duplicating mitigations or herd preferences** outside this file / `GOALS.md` — keep functional mitigations here; keep owner targets in goals.

## Optional Young Stock program (booklet status)

As of the 2019 booklet text, Optional Young Stock Appraisal was **suspended**. Historically: subset of linears; stature, rump width, and escutcheon height in inches; final letter rating (not numeric Final Score); not used in sire summaries.
