# DAIRY_CONCEPTS.md
**ADGA Linear Appraisal concepts, trait scales, and terminology**  
**Authoritative source:** [ADGA Linear Appraisal SOP (Revised 3/2025)](https://adga.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025Linear-SOPDraft1.pdf)  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-28  

Authoritative reference for how Linear Appraisal (LA) traits are defined, measured, and scored.  
**Use this file whenever writing or interpreting LA reports** so trait polarity, measurement method, and terminology stay consistent with ADGA — do not invent scales or reverse biological extremes.

Where the SOP is silent on educational biological anchors (e.g. intermediate verbal landmarks) but the ADGA Unified Scorecard (SOP Appendix C) or long-standing LA education language is consistent with the SOP, that language may be retained. **If the SOP and any older booklet conflict, the SOP wins.**

Companion files (in a herd repo that uses this concepts file):
- Report rules: `LA_REPORT_GUIDELINES.md`
- Herd breeding goals / preferences: `GOALS.md` (herd-specific — not defined here)
- Score tables: whatever LA score files that herd maintains

## How to use this document

1. Before describing a trait as “good,” “weak,” “flat,” “steep,” “high,” or “low,” check the scale and biological meaning below.
2. Prefer **full ADGA trait names** in prose (e.g. Medial Suspensory Ligament, Rear Legs Side View). Abbreviations are fine in tables and Final Score shorthand (`VEVG 88`).
3. Linear scores describe position on a biological range. For most primary traits that is **1–50**. They do **not** by themselves declare a point “ideal” (SOP §2.4).
4. If this file and a prior report conflict on trait polarity or measurement method, **this file wins**; correct the report.
5. Follow the **language rules** below (especially breeding terminology).

## Language rules

### Breeding — not mating

In all reports, prompts, and docs:

- Use **breeding** / **breedings** — never **mating** / **matings**.
- Use the verb **breed** — never **mate**.
- Prefer phrases like: breeding, proposed breeding, recommended breeding, breeding pair, breeding choice, breed to, breed with.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| mating | breeding |
| matings | breedings |
| mate (verb) | breed |
| mate her to X | breed her to X |
| recommended mating | recommended breeding |
| prospective mating | prospective breeding |
| test mating | test breeding |

These words are house style even if other sources say “mating.”

## System overview

Per the **2025 Linear Appraisal SOP** (§2):

- Evaluates individual type traits that affect structural and functional durability.
- Three parts: **linear traits**, **structural/functional characteristics**, and **major scorecard categories** (including Final Score).
- **15 primary linear traits** scored to describe the biological range for that trait. Most use **1–50**; **Rear Udder Side View** uses **0–4** (SOP §14.11). The description does **not** set a certain point as “ideal,” nor are more, fewer, or mid-range points inherently more desirable (SOP §2.4).
- **Eight structural/functional areas:** head, shoulder assembly, front legs, rear legs, feet, back, rump, and udder texture — letter-rated **P / F / A / G / V / E** (SOP §2.5).
- **Major categories:** General Appearance, Dairy Strength, Body Capacity, and Mammary System (Mammary = does; three categories for bucks), plus Final Score.
- Miscellaneous codes / remarks provide additional herd-owner information (functional codes publish in performance media).

**Genetic evaluation / sire summaries:** Primary linear trait data **plus Final Score** feed USDA/CDCB algorithms for sire summaries (SOP §2.4). Structural letters and most remarks are for management/promotion.

**Scoring adjustments (SOP §10.4–10.5):** Linear and structural categories are evaluated **without** adjusting for stage of lactation or number of lactations; those adjustments are accounted for in the **major categories** and Final Score. Age adjustment for major categories/Final Score applies to animals **seven years and older**. Does must generally have freshened at least twice to be Excellent overall (exception: first freshener in milk ≥365 days with verification path). Bucks must be **2+ years** before Excellent (90+) overall.

**High-score verification (SOP §16):** Final Scores meeting or exceeding **93** follow a verification procedure (photos/video, PPM review). On-farm worksheet shows customary letter scores; projected scores above 93 are not disclosed to the owner that day.

## Score types at a glance

| Kind | What it is | Scale |
|------|------------|--------|
| Primary **linear traits** | Observed biological position | Usually **1–50**; RUSV **0–4** |
| **Structural / functional areas** | Head, shoulders, legs, feet, back, rump, udder texture | Letter: **E / V / G / A / F / P** |
| **Major categories** | General Appearance, Dairy Strength, Body Capacity, Mammary | Same letters (Mammary = does) |
| **Final Score** | Overall closeness to functional ideal | Numeric for appraised animals (except Poor = `P` in some contexts) |

### Letter ratings (structural areas & major categories)

Official letters per SOP §2.5 / §15 (Good Plus = **G**):

| Letter | Name | Numeric band (Final Score / category function) |
|--------|------|------------------------------------------------|
| **E** | Excellent | ≥ 90 |
| **V** | Very Good | 85–89 |
| **G** | Good Plus | 80–84 |
| **A** | Acceptable | 70–79 |
| **F** | Fair | 60–69 |
| **P** | Poor | ≤ 59 |

Herd score exports and older materials sometimes print Good Plus as **`+`**. Treat `G` and `+` as the same Good Plus band when composing Final Score shorthand (e.g. `VEEG 85` = V / E / E / G).

### Function bands (SOP §15)

**Function:** ability to be productive, relative to age, without significant detriment to strength, soundness, dairy form, or mobility into reasonable maturity.

| Band | Meaning (summary) |
|------|-------------------|
| **Poor (≤59)** | Inability to function; severe structural defects in multiple categories |
| **Fair (60–69)** | Cannot sustain function through maturity, or loses function with maturity |
| **Acceptable (70–79)** | Adequate longevity/productivity; moderate function-related defects |
| **Good Plus (80–84)** | Potential for or evidence of commendable lifetime performance; minor defects |
| **Very Good (85–89)** | Form for high-level function over an extended period |
| **Excellent (90+)** | Exceptional quality with proven productivity path (see sub-levels below) |

**Excellent sub-levels (SOP §15.F):** 90 = exceptional in many categories with second lactation (or verified 365-day first lactation); 91 = extremely correct, ease of movement; 92 = more extreme exceptional traits / balance; 93 = elite dairy form, structure, strength, capacity, mammary; 94 = exceptional movement and correlation of parts in all categories; 95–100 = elite beyond prior Excellent levels (requires verifiable production evidence).

### Final Score contribution (ADGA Unified Scorecard weights — SOP Appendix C)

**Mature does** (any doe that has ever freshened):

| Category | Weight |
|----------|--------|
| General Appearance | 35% |
| Dairy Strength | 20% |
| Body Capacity | 10% |
| Mammary | 35% |

**Bucks and young stock (scorecard):**

| Category | Weight |
|----------|--------|
| General Appearance | 55% |
| Dairy Strength | 30% |
| Body Capacity | 15% |
| Mammary | *(not used)* |

Permanent-score and related program rules follow the current **ADGA Guidebook, Article X** (referenced by the SOP).

## Primary linear traits

Fifteen primary traits (SOP §2.4). Measurement and scoring notes below follow SOP §§11–14 and Appendix A scales.

### Form — Stature

**Meaning:** Height at the **highest point of the withers** (SOP §11.2). Front legs square under withers; rear legs set so an imaginary line from pin to ground passes through the hock; solid level surface; head alert (not stretched or pushed).

**SOP rule:** Stature is **measured and recorded as a linear scale on all goats** [2024] (SOP §11.2.A). Animals with Final Score ≥93, and any animal with MC **82** (over-height), also get an exact inch measurement recorded (SOP §5.1).

**Linear scale — stature (SOP Appendix A):**

| Standard inches | Miniature inches | Linear score |
|----------------:|-----------------:|-------------:|
| ≤26 | 17 | 5 |
| 27 | 18 | 10 |
| 28 | 19 | 15 |
| 29 | 20 | 20 |
| 30 | 21 | 25 |
| 31 | 22 | 30 |
| 32 | 23 | 35 |
| 33 | 24 | 40 |
| 34 | 25 | 45 |

Interpolate between listed heights (~5 points per 1″ on either scale). Over-height miniatures: GA and Final Score should reflect that; assign **MC 82**. Mature (4+) animals under minimum height that are otherwise exceptional may still be Excellent overall/GA; reference **MC 83** (SOP §11.2.E–F).

When owner height is known for a miniature, convert with this table and compare to the official LA stature score.

---

### Form — Strength

**Meaning:** Depth and width of chest in **front view**, from extremely narrow/frail to extremely wide/strong (SOP §12.2).

**Polarity:** Low = narrow/frail; high = wide/strong.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| 5 | Extremely narrow and frail |
| 15 | Narrow and frail |
| 25 | Intermediate width and strength |
| 35 | Very wide and strong |
| 45 | Extremely wide and strong |

SOP bands [2024]: weakness typically **&lt;20**; strength typically **&gt;30**; intermediate between.

---

### Form — Dairyness

**Meaning:** Visual evaluation in **side view** — flatness of bone and openness of rib (SOP §12.1). Appraiser may handle the animal to clarify placement.

**Polarity:** Low = round/tight-ribbed; high = flat-boned/open-ribbed.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Extremely round boned and tight ribbed |
| 15 | Thick / rounder bone, tighter rib |
| 25 | Intermediate angularity and openness |
| 35 | Sharp / flat bone, open rib |
| ≥45 | Extremely flat boned and open ribbed |

**SOP [2024]:** Round-boned, tight-ribbed animals can only score **Fair or Poor** in the Dairy Strength **major category**.

---

### Structure — Rump Angle

**Meaning:** Angle of the rump/pelvis **from hips to pins**, viewed from the side (goat on the move). Scorecard ideal language: nearly level from hips to pinbones. Affects kidding ease and reproductive-tract drainage; also related to udder length fore-to-rear, fore udder attachment, and udder depth.

**Polarity (critical):**
- **Low scores = steep** (pins drop more relative to hips)
- **High scores = level / flat** (toward pins level with hips; extreme levelness can approach inverted)

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 / 5 | Extremely steep from hips to pins |
| 15 | Considerable slope from hips to pins |
| 25 | Moderate slope from hips to pins |
| 35 | Nearly level from hips to pins |
| ≥45 / 45 | Extremely level from hips to pins |

Educational anchors consistent with scorecard polarity: steepness often **≤20**; levelness often **≥30**; intermediate slopes roughly **20–30**. About **5 points per 5°** change. A physical rump angle of **50° or more** is assigned **1 point** (extreme steep).

**Report language:**
- Say **steep** for low RA scores; say **level / flat** for high RA scores.
- “Further **flattening**” means moving the score **up** toward 50.

---

### Structure — Rump Width

**Meaning:** Width between the **thurls**, measured from the rear from the top outermost portion of the bone (not in the hollow), with digital calipers (SOP §11.4). Related to kidding ease, body width, and potential udder width.

**Polarity:** Low = narrow; high = wide.

**Linear scale — rump width (SOP Appendix A):**

| Standard inches | Miniature inches | Linear score |
|----------------:|-----------------:|-------------:|
| ≤5 | 4.5 | 5 |
| 5.5 | 4.75 | 10 |
| 6 | 5 | 15 |
| 6.5 | 5.25 | 20 |
| 7 | 5.5 | 25 |
| 7.5 | 5.75 | 30 |
| 8 | 6 | 35 |
| 8.5 | 6.25 | 40 |
| 9 | 6.5 | 45 |

---

### Structure — Rear Legs, Side View

**Meaning:** Degree of angulation at the hock (side view). Evaluated when the animal comes to a stop and rear legs are fully weight-bearing (SOP §11.3). Relates to durability of legs and feet.

**Polarity:**
- **Low = posty / straight** in the hock
- **High = sickled / more angled**

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| 5 | Straight-legged (posty) in hock |
| 15 | Nearly straight (posty) |
| 25 | Intermediate angle |
| 35 | Considerably angled (sickled) |
| 45 | Extremely angled (sickled) |

Posty tendency often **≤20**; greater angulation often **≥30**. Extremes either way can be functional risks.

---

### Mammary — Fore Udder Attachment

**Meaning:** Strength of lateral ligaments extending forward/laterally to the body wall. SOP §14.6: fore-udder evaluation considers extension of udder forward of the teats, extension of the lateral, and width across the lateral, without regard to position of udder on the body wall.

**Polarity:** Low = loose; high = snug/strong.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Extremely loose attachment |
| 15 | Loose attachment |
| 25 | Intermediate strength |
| 35 | Strong attachment |
| 45 | Extremely snug and strong attachment |

---

### Mammary — Rear Udder Height

**Meaning:** Height of rear udder attachment relative to the body. **Not scored on bucks** (SOP §14.10.A).

**SOP method (critical — multi-size breeds):** Absolute inch conversion is **not** used. The appraiser visually assesses secretory tissue height relative to the span from **point of hock** to **base of pelvic arch**:

| Landmark | Linear score |
|----------|-------------:|
| Midpoint (halfway pelvic arch ↔ hock) | **10** |
| ⅝ above hock | **20** |
| ¾ above hock | **30** |
| ⅞ above hock | **40** |
| At pelvic arch | **50** |

**Polarity:** Low = low attachment; high = high attachment.

Hair/skin texture differences between rear udder and escutcheon help locate the attachment.

---

### Mammary — Rear Udder Arch

**Meaning:** Width and shape of the rear udder attachment at the same location where height is assessed (SOP §14.9). If the udder is carried too far forward, evaluate on the move.

**Polarity:** Low = narrow/pointed; high = wide/curving.

**SOP landmark:** If the arch still has the look of a **point** at center-top → **19 or less**. Once **rounded** → **20 and over**.

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| ≤5 | Extremely narrow and pointed arch |
| 15 | Narrow and pointed arch |
| 25 | Intermediate width and curve |
| 35 | Wide and curving arch |
| ≥45 | Extremely wide and curving arch |

---

### Mammary — Medial Suspensory Ligament (MSL)

**Meaning:** Primary support of the udder. Strong MSL keeps teats placed and udder elevated. Scored from cleft in the floor of the udder attributable to the MSL. Uneven halves: use the functional-appearing half (SOP §14.3).

**Polarity:** Low = weak support / bulging or negative cleft; high = deep cleft / strong support.

**Linear scale — medial (SOP Appendix A):**

| Standard inches (cleft) | Miniature inches | Linear score |
|------------------------:|-----------------:|-------------:|
| −1 | −½ | 5 |
| 0 | 0 | 15 |
| 1 | ½ | 25 |
| 2 | 1 | 35 |
| 3 | 1½ | 45 |

**Higher MSL is generally desirable** for support and longevity (within functional extremes).

---

### Mammary — Udder Depth

**Meaning:** Vertical distance between udder floor and point of hock, viewed at eye level, rear leg set normally (SOP §14.8). Some depth is needed for capacity; extreme depth raises injury/mastitis risk. Uneven halves: use the functional-appearing half (SOP §14.3).

**Polarity (important):**  
- **Low scores = deep / pendulous** (floor toward or below hocks)  
- **High scores = shallow / high** (floor well above hocks)

**Linear scale — udder depth (SOP Appendix A)** — inches above (+) or below (−) hock:

| Standard inches | Miniature inches | Linear score |
|----------------:|-----------------:|-------------:|
| ≤ −2 | −1 | 5 |
| −1 | −½ | 10 |
| 0 | 0 | 15 |
| 1 | ½ | 20 |
| 2 | 1 | 25 |
| 3 | 1½ | 30 |
| 4 | 2 | 35 |
| 5 | 2½ | 40 |
| 6 | 3 | 45 |
| 7 | 3½ | 50 |

Do not call a high Udder Depth score “deep.”

---

### Mammary — Teat Placement, Rear View

**Meaning:** Teat location on the udder half from the rear (ease of milking; injury risk). Position = center of teat at attachment.

**Polarity:** Low = too far to the outside; high = closer toward midline (more ideal placement on the half).

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| 5 | Extremely wide — outside of udder half |
| 15 | On outside of udder half |
| 25 | About ⅔ of the way out on the udder half |
| 35 | Midway between ⅓ and ⅔ out |
| 45 | About ⅓ of the way out on the udder half |

Outside-third placements are typically **&lt;25** (**wide** placement); more medial useful placements **&gt;25** (**narrower / close** to midline on the half).

**Report language:** Use **wide** and **narrow** (or **close**) for Teat Placement.

---

### Mammary — Teat Diameter

**Meaning:** Diameter at the base where teat meets udder (rear view). Reflects delineation and ease of milking.

**Polarity:** Low = narrow; high = wide. Extremes either way can be undesirable for function.

**SOP Appendix A:**  
- Very narrow: ≤ **½″** standard / ≤ **¼″** miniature → **≤5**  
- Intermediate: about **1½″** standard (proportionate miniature) → **25**  
- Very wide: ≥ **2½″** standard / ≥ **1¼″** miniature → **≥45**  
- Step: **½″** standard / **¼″** miniature ≈ **10 points**

---

### Mammary — Teat Length

**Meaning:** Length from the base (where teat diameter is assessed) to the end of the teat, evaluated from the rear at eye level or suitable distance; side profile if needed (SOP §14.12). Visual assessment converting inches to the linear scale (data collection since 2015).

**Polarity:** Low = short; high = long. Extremes either way can be undesirable (misc codes 56/57).

**Linear scale — teat length (SOP Appendix A):**

| Standard inches | Miniature inches | Linear score |
|----------------:|-----------------:|-------------:|
| 0.5 | 0.25 | 5 |
| 1.0 | 0.5 | 10 |
| 1.5 | 0.75 | 15 |
| 2.0 | 1.0 | 20 |
| 2.5 | 1.25 | 25 |
| 3.0 | 1.5 | 30 |
| 3.5 | 1.75 | 35 |
| 4.0 | 2.0 | 40 |
| 4.5 | 2.25 | 45 |
| 5.0 | 2.5 | 50 |

---

### Mammary — Rear Udder, Side View (RUSV)

**Meaning:** Shape of the rear udder extending behind the rear leg when legs are square beneath the doe. Indicates rear-udder milk capacity.

**Scale (SOP §14.11):** **0–4** (not 1–50).

| Score | Description |
|------:|-------------|
| **0–1** | Extremely flat; little capacity (0 = no udder behind the leg) |
| **2–3** | Intermediate fullness |
| **4** | Extremely rounded, bulging, or protruding (beyond the vulva) |

**Polarity:** Low = flat / little capacity; high = rounded / bulging / protruding. Intermediate fullness is often the practical target; avoid extremes.

## Major categories (what appraisers consider)

Aligned with SOP §§12–14 and Unified Scorecard (Appendix C).

### General Appearance (GA)
Total structure: head, shoulder blades, back, loin, rump, legs, feet. Attractive blending, vigor, style, carriage, walk. Strength, upstandingness, stretch, sound feet/legs, level topline, wide/level rump, smooth blending support E/V.

### Dairy Strength (DS)
Bone structure (neck, withers, ribs, thighs), flanks, angularity, openness, fleshing, animation, skin/hair. Sharp, angular, refined bone, lean blending neck, open ribbing, incurving thighs → E/V. Round bone / tight rib → **F/P only** in this category (SOP §12.1).

### Body Capacity
Length, width, depth with correct shape (feed capacity). Deep supported barrel, sprung ribs widening rearward, large heart girth, wide chest floor → E/V. Narrow/pinched girth, frailness, shallow or cramped ribbing → **F/P** (SOP §13.1). Body depth assessed spine-to-barrel at last rib; very deep ≥45, very shallow ≤5 on the body-depth continuum (SOP §13.2).

### Mammary System (does)
Capacity and shape; rear and fore attachments; texture; teats. Capacious, strongly attached, well-carried, quality udder for production and longevity. Soft, pliable texture; uniform, well-placed teats. Short/bulgy/loose fore, low/narrow/loose/pinched rear, tied/twisted/pendulous → may be **F/P** (SOP §14.7).

## Functional risks & mitigations

Use this section when a breeding or selection package includes an undesirable extreme or a known functional concern. **Herd target bands and owner preferences belong in that herd’s `GOALS.md`** — this section only states biological/functional risks and usual mitigations.

### Rump Angle

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Very steep** (often ≤20) | Can affect kidding ease, reproductive-tract drainage, and related udder topography | Select toward a more moderate/level angle over generations; pair with adequate **Rump Width** for pelvic capacity |
| **Very level / flat** (often ≥30; extreme toward inverted) | Relatively level pelves can increase kidding difficulty, especially if the pelvis is also narrow | Prefer adequate-to-wide **Rump Width** (thurls) when angle is in the level range; avoid stacking extreme flatness with narrow rumps |
| **Moving steep → more level** | Often desirable structurally when leaving an extreme steep | Treat as improvement toward moderation unless the herd’s goals say otherwise; do **not** invent a risk solely because the score rose from steep toward moderate/level |
| **Further flattening** when already very level | Harder to reverse; can compound kidding risk | Avoid partners that push angle still higher; maintain or improve width |

**Rump Width** is always useful for pelvic capacity. It is also the usual mitigation for kidding risk when Rump Angle is relatively level (~≥30) or flatter.

### Rump Width

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Narrow** | Limits pelvic capacity; weaker kidding buffer; may limit udder width potential | Prefer wider partners; avoid stacking two narrow rumps |

### Medial Suspensory Ligament

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Weak / low cleft** | Udder support fails earlier; teats displace; longevity and Final Score suffer | Prefer partners with stronger MSL (or proven mammary transmitting); avoid stacking two weak-MSL animals |

### Teat Placement (rear) & Teat Diameter / Length

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Wide placement** (low score — too far outside) | Harder milking; higher injury risk | Prefer partners with closer/more medial placement; avoid stacking wide×wide |
| **Extremely narrow or extremely wide diameter** | Milking difficulty; injury or machine fit issues | Prefer intermediate diameters; don’t stack extremes |
| **Extremely short or long teats** | Milking / injury issues (codes 56/57) | Prefer intermediate length for the breed; don’t stack extremes |

### Udder Depth

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Very deep / pendulous** (low score) | Injury, mastitis, and longevity risk | Prefer partners that carry udders higher; strong MSL and attachments help hold shape |

### Rear Legs — Side View

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Posty or sickled extremes** | Foot/leg durability over time (low heritability — slow change) | Prefer intermediate set; track multi-generationally rather than expecting one breeding to “fix” it |

### Strength / Dairyness extremes

| Concern | Why it matters | Usual mitigation / counterbalance |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Very frail (low Strength)** | Harder to sustain production and general thrift | Prefer stronger partners; don’t stack frail×frail |
| **Very coarse (low Dairyness)** | Score and dairy character suffer; DS category capped at F/P when round/tight-ribbed | Prefer sharper, cleaner-boned, open-ribbed partners |

### How reports should use this

1. Name the trait risk in plain language (using correct polarity from this file).  
2. Cite the mitigation from this table when one exists.  
3. Apply **herd preferences** (target bands, animals to protect) only from that herd’s `GOALS.md` / roster notes — do not invent them here.

## Quick polarity cheat sheet

| Trait | Low score means | High score means | “Better” direction (typical) |
|-------|-----------------|------------------|------------------------------|
| Stature | Short | Tall | Breed/goals dependent (miniatures!) |
| Strength | Frail/narrow | Wide/strong | Higher usually preferred |
| Dairyness | Coarse/round/tight rib | Flat bone/open rib | Higher usually preferred |
| **Rump Angle** | **Steep** | **Level / flat** | Avoid extremes; flattening = **up** |
| Rump Width | Narrow | Wide | Higher usually preferred |
| Rear Legs Side View | Posty | Sickled | Intermediate often preferred |
| Fore Udder Attachment | Loose | Snug/strong | Higher usually preferred |
| Rear Udder Height | Low (near hock) | High (toward pelvic arch) | Higher usually preferred |
| Rear Udder Arch | Narrow/pointed (≤19 pointed) | Wide/curved (≥20 rounded) | Higher usually preferred |
| Medial Suspensory Ligament | Weak / negative cleft | Deep cleft / support | Higher usually preferred |
| **Udder Depth** | **Deep / pendulous** | **Shallow / high** | Avoid extreme depth (low) |
| Teat Placement Rear | Too far out | More medial on half | Higher usually preferred |
| Teat Diameter | Narrow | Wide | Intermediate often preferred |
| Teat Length | Short | Long | Intermediate often preferred |
| Rear Udder Side View (0–4) | Flat / low capacity | Bulging / protruding | Intermediate–full; avoid extremes |

## Common hallucination pitfalls (do not do these)

1. **Reversing Rump Angle** — low ≠ flat; high ≠ steep.
2. **Reversing Udder Depth** — high score ≠ deep udder.
3. **Treating every linear as “higher is always better”** — SOP §2.4: linears describe; they do not define ideal by themselves.
4. **Confusing structural Rump letter** with linear Rump Angle / Rump Width.
5. **Assuming bucks have mammary linear scores** — RUH and other mammary linears are not recorded on bucks as applicable.
6. **Inventing heritabilities** — use the herd’s `GOALS.md` priorities rather than fabricating numbers.
7. **Ignoring miniature scales** — stature, rump width, udder depth, MSL, teat diameter, and teat length have official miniature inch→score tables in SOP Appendix A.
8. **Treating RUSV as a 1–50 trait** — it is **0–4**.
9. **Using absolute-inch RUH conversion** — mature RUH is body-relative (midpoint = 10, ¾ = 30, etc.).
10. **Using `+` as the only Good Plus letter** — official SOP letter is **G** (`+` on older exports = same band).
11. **Duplicating mitigations or herd preferences** outside this file / `GOALS.md`.
